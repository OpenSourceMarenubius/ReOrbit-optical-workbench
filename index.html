UNDER THE HOOD

A workbench with honest boundaries.

ReOrbit v0.1 traces 3D rays through spherical, conic/even-aspheric, and tilted planar refractive surfaces using vector Snell refraction. Spot diagrams use 401 uniform-area pupil samples for collimated input, or uniform-solid-angle samples for a finite point source, at one wavelength. Layout rays are a separate meridional sample.

Best focus minimises centroid-referenced geometric RMS radius for the surviving sampled rays. It is not a general lens optimiser. Image distance is measured from the last surface vertex. “To next” is ignored on the final row.

The searchable glass catalogue uses SCHOTT Sellmeier coefficients. AIR is fixed at n = 1. Wavelengths are limited to 380–1700 nm. No absorption, coatings, Fresnel power, polarisation, diffraction, wavefront/MTF, thermal effects, extended emitters, mechanical tolerances, or non-sequential paths are modelled.

This is an early engineering prototype. Cross-check prescriptions against an independent optical tool and physical measurements before fabrication. Example prescriptions are illustrative, not qualified optical assemblies.

LIGHT SOURCE

Control the light.

Fill first lens centres the emission direction and adjusts the cone to reach both aperture edges. Layout rays are spaced evenly across the illuminated part of the first lens; spot calculations retain uniform angular sampling.

Direction 0° points along the optical axis; positive angles tilt upward in the layout. A 5° half-angle produces a 10° full cone. Changes update the trace immediately.

      SURFACE GEOMETRY

      Surface shape

      Mirror faces retain the incoming medium. A negative radius is concave toward the incoming axis. The next surface and image plane follow the nominal reflected axis; spacings stay positive along that path.

      k = 0: sphere · k = −1: paraboloid. Sag is the base conic plus A2·r² + A4·r⁴ + … + A16·r¹⁶. Radius stays in the prescription table.

      Positive tilt slopes the face toward +z at positive transverse height: z = vertex + tan(tilt)·x. Radius is set to 0. Clear diameter is projected onto the transverse plane. Side walls and automatic internal-reflection paths are not traced. Explicit mirror surfaces reflect along the prescribed sequence.

      GEOMETRIC BEAM DIAGNOSTICS

      What the rays can tell us.

      These values can reveal clipping, residual geometric divergence and the effects of changing source/lens spacing, glass or mirror folds.

      Angular RMS is measured about the mean ray direction. The envelope diameter is a sampled geometric boundary, not a Gaussian 1/e² beam diameter. Ray survival is not optical power transmission.

      Real transmitter predictions still need emitter size and fast/slow-axis profiles, M², collimator and lens prescriptions, coating transmission, and measurements. The current model does not calculate diffraction, electrical drive, emitted power, BER or atmospheric link range.
